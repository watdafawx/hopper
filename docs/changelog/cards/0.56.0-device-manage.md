# Let the owner allow a paired PC to manage the panel

**Branch:** claude/device-manage   **Commit:** bc7101c   **Size:** M
**Apps:** both (Hopper Server: tick box, device toggle, rights. Desktop: wording and the check; no new desktop screen, the desktop shows no pairing role).

## From -> to
- Before: a PC paired through the approval flow was always a plain "device". Deleting or removing a server on the panel was refused, and the desktop said "this PC is paired as a device. Delete it in the panel: Settings > General > Delete server." even for the owner.
- After: when the owner approves a PC they can tick "Let this PC manage the panel (delete servers, change users)" (off by default). They can also switch it on or off later per device in Settings > Access & devices. A PC with it on gets manager-level rights and the desktop shows the normal delete dialog. A plain PC is told what to do: "This PC can't delete servers on <host>. In <host>'s panel, open Settings > Access & devices and allow this PC to manage it, or delete the server there." (same words for Remove, with "remove").

## What the flag can and cannot do
- Stored on the device (`manage`, default false; old `auth.json` loads as off). Not a new role: `/auth/me` still says `"role":"device"` plus `"manage":true/false`. Audit log role stays "device".
- Can: everything a manager's `owner_level()` / `require_owner` allows, i.e. delete/remove servers, users (add/change/remove non-managers), tokens, webhooks, audit log, panel settings, devices list, config-sync (owner-level parts), pairing list.
- Cannot (checked on a scratch panel, all 403): turn the flag on/off for itself or any device (`PUT /devices/{id}/manage` is true-owner only), tick it when approving a request (manager or device asking = 403), approve or refuse any pairing at all (a device never lets other PCs in), remove devices (`may_forget_device` is false for devices), add/change/remove managers, change the owner's password or two-factor.
- Unchanged: plain devices, tokens, users, managers, the owner; path checks, rate limits, setup code, TLS.
- Routes a flagged device gains (all that call `require_owner` / `require_owner_who` / `owner_level`): users (list/create/update/delete/reset-2fa, non-managers only), tokens (list/create/revoke), audit, pair/pending, devices (list), servers delete/remove, settings, webhooks, addresses, certificate, config-sync owner parts. The grant route and pairing answer have extra true-owner / non-device checks.
- Audit: the approval and every toggle appear in the audit log with who did it, e.g. `owner/owner PUT /api/v1/devices/<id>/manage (allowed to manage the panel)` and `POST /api/v1/pair/<id>/answer (allowed to manage the panel)`.
- Shown to devices: the device list still serialises each device's token hash (as before, to owner-level callers). Now a flagged device sees other devices' hashes too. They are SHA-256 of a 128-bit random token, so not usable, but worth removing later.
